1 · Quick overview

What is the UHP-400?

The UHP-400 is a benchtop probe-type ultrasonic homogenizer (probe sonicator) for laboratories. It drives a titanium probe at a fixed 24 kHz with up to 400 W of electrical power from its generator, and treats liquid samples up to 3.5 L. The probe, a height-adjustable sample table, a temperature sensor and a touchscreen controller are built into one sound cabinet with a window. Labs use it for cell lysis, nanoparticle dispersion, emulsification and plant extraction. It is designed and made by Topsonics in Vilnius, Lithuania.

About the 400 W figure

400 W is the maximum electrical power the generator delivers to the transducer [confirm]. It is not the power drawn from the wall socket, and it is not the acoustic power that reaches your sample.

The mains draw is higher than the output because the generator and its cooling fan have losses. The power that goes into the liquid is lower again, and depends on the probe, the amplitude and the liquid. We state it separately, measured by calorimetry: the temperature rise of a known volume of water over a short run.

3 · For researchers

What the literature says

Six sample-preparation problems, and what each means at the bench

Each card summarises a peer-reviewed, open-access paper and then says what it means in practice. These studies used their own equipment, not the UHP-400; they show why the settings matter, not how our instrument performed.

Nanoparticles clump together

Metal nanoparticles agglomerate strongly in liquid. In this study, probe sonication dispersed them better than an ultrasonic bath.

In practice: Use a probe, not a bath, for agglomerated powders, and report the power and time you used.

Pradhan et al., 2016 · J. Nanoparticle Research 18:285Figure 1 from the paper, CC BY 4.0

Results are hard to reproduce

A 2025 review found that changes in sonication method, power and duration change particle properties and the toxicity results that follow.

In practice: Write power, pulse, time, volume and probe size into your method. On the UHP-400 these are numbers you can save and reload.

McCormack et al., 2025 · Particle and Fibre Toxicology 22Figure 3 from the paper, CC BY 4.0

Too much energy damages the sample

The sonication needed to shear chromatin often degraded large proteins, and runs under identical-looking conditions gave different results.

In practice: Use pulse mode and an ice bath for proteins and chromatin. The UHP-400 stops the run at the temperature limit you set.

Pchelintsev et al., 2016 · PLoS ONE 11:e0148023Figure 3 from the paper, CC BY 4.0

Extraction takes too long

Compared with maceration, ultrasound-assisted extraction gave significantly higher yields of phenolic acids in a shorter time.

In practice: Test short runs first; ultrasound can replace hours of maceration. One UHP-400 cycle runs up to 30 minutes.

Momchev et al., 2020 · Molecules 25:5142Figure 1 from the paper, CC BY 4.0

Solvents cost money and add risk

Pulsed ultrasound-assisted extraction with a food-grade solvent was tested as a greener alternative to conventional maceration.

In practice: Pulsed runs with a milder solvent are worth testing. The UHP-400 duty cycle goes from 5 to 95 %.

Turrini et al., 2019 · Foods 8:466Figure 3 from the paper, CC BY 4.0

Cell lysis is hard to get right

Too little energy leaves cells intact and too much degrades proteins. The best energy input changed with the experiment.

In practice: Find the energy that works for your cells and repeat it. The UHP-400 records the energy of every run.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to what labs ask us most about probe sonication and the UHP-400.

Which probe should I use for my sample volume?

Use the 15 mm probe for batches from 100 mL up to 3.5 L, and the 7 mm probe for smaller volumes such as vials, tubes and small beakers. A thinner tip concentrates the power on a smaller area, so it gives a small sample a higher intensity; the wider tip spreads the energy through a larger volume. Both probes are Ti-6Al-4V titanium and fit the same acoustic stack.

Is 400 W the power that goes into my sample?

No. 400 W is the maximum electrical power the generator delivers to the transducer [confirm]. The power drawn from the wall socket is higher, because the generator and its fan have losses. The acoustic power that reaches the liquid is lower, and depends on the probe, the amplitude and the liquid; it is measured by calorimetry, from the temperature rise of a known volume of water. See the specification table for our measured values [values to add].

How do I keep the sample from overheating during sonication?

Combine pulse mode, cooling and a temperature limit. On the UHP-400 the pulse duty cycle is adjustable from 5 to 95 % of a 15 s period, so the sample cools between bursts. Put heat-sensitive samples such as proteins or cells in an ice bath. The built-in sensor reads 0–80 °C and stops the run automatically when your limit is reached.

How deep should the probe tip go into the liquid?

Immerse the tip about 10–15 mm below the surface and keep it centred, at least one probe diameter away from the vessel wall and bottom. A tip that is too shallow pulls in air and causes foaming; a tip that touches the wall wastes energy and can crack glass. The adjustable sample table lets you repeat the same position on every run.

Ultrasonic homogenizer or rotor-stator: which is better?

For small and medium liquid batches an ultrasonic homogenizer usually reaches smaller particle and droplet sizes and breaks cells more completely. The probe is one solid piece with no seals or blades, so it is quick to clean, and you can switch probes for different volumes. Its drawback is heat, which the UHP-400 controls with pulse mode and the automatic temperature limit. Rotor-stator mixers suit larger volumes and very viscous material.

How do I look after the titanium probe, and how long does it last?

Rinse the tip with deionized water after each run, wipe it dry and avoid abrasive cleaners. Check the end face regularly: cavitation slowly erodes it into a rough, pitted surface. Replace a worn or cracked tip, because it loses efficiency and puts extra load on the transducer. Probe life depends on power, run time and the sample; abrasive particles and solvents wear it faster.

What is the UHP-400 used for?

The main uses are cell lysis (bacteria, yeast and tissue) for protein and DNA work, emulsification, dispersing and de-agglomerating nanoparticles, and extracting compounds from plant material. It is also used to shear chromatin for ChIP.

Is the UHP-400 loud? Do I need a separate sound box?

No separate box is needed. The UHP-400 has a built-in sound cabinet with a door and window, so the probe runs enclosed and you can watch the sample through the window. [Measured at 1 m: ___ dB(A) door closed, ___ dB(A) door open]
